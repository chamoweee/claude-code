Understanding the diet

What does “no allium” actually mean?

The allium family includes onion, garlic, leek, shallot, scallion, and chive. Recipes on Sattva Table skip all of them — while staying 100% vegetarian.

Where it comes from

A tradition, not a trend

🕉️

Sattvic diet (Yoga & Ayurveda)

In Ayurveda, foods are classed as sattvic (pure, calming), rajasic (stimulating), or tamasic (dulling). Onion and garlic are considered rajasic — they're avoided by many practitioners seeking a clear, calm mind for meditation and yoga.

☸️

Jain cuisine

Jain dietary principles emphasize non-violence (ahimsa) toward all living things, including root vegetables whose harvest can harm the plant and surrounding organisms. Onion, garlic, and other root vegetables are excluded entirely.

🛕

Swaminarayan & other Hindu traditions

Many Hindu temple kitchens and Swaminarayan households avoid onion and garlic as part of a sattvic offering (prasad) tradition, believing simpler foods keep devotional practice pure.

🩺

Low-FODMAP & digestive sensitivity

Onion and garlic are among the highest-FODMAP foods, common triggers for IBS and bloating. Allium-free cooking has become a practical choice for digestive comfort, independent of any religious practice.

In the kitchen

What we use instead

These swaps recreate the savory backbone onion and garlic usually provide.

Instead of… We use… Why it works
Garlic Asafoetida (hing), toasted in hot oil A pungent, savory resin that mimics garlic's depth when bloomed in fat
Onion (for sweetness/body) Grated ginger, cabbage, or bottle gourd Adds moisture and mild sweetness to a base without the sharp bite of onion
Onion (for aromatics) Cumin, mustard seeds, curry leaves bloomed in oil (tadka) Builds a fragrant, savory foundation at the start of cooking
Garlic (raw, for punch) Fresh ginger + green chili Delivers heat and brightness in place of raw garlic's sharpness
Leeks/shallots Fennel bulb or celery, finely diced Provides a similar mild, aromatic crunch in stir-fries and stocks

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Is no-allium cooking the same as low-FODMAP?

Not exactly — low-FODMAP excludes a wider list of foods (like certain fruits and dairy). But since onion and garlic are the biggest FODMAP triggers for most people, our recipes are naturally a great starting point.

Can I still use asafoetida if I'm strictly Jain?

Most Jain households do use asafoetida (hing) as a garlic substitute, though some brands are cut with wheat flour or other fillers — check the label if you have additional restrictions.

Do you use root vegetables like potato and carrot?

Some recipes do. We label strictly Jain-friendly recipes (which also avoid root vegetables) separately in each recipe's tags where relevant.

Is everything on this site vegetarian?

Yes — every recipe on Sattva Table is 100% vegetarian. Many are also egg-free; dairy is used where relevant (ghee, yogurt, milk).
